# Opening-line patterns of top English explainer Shorts creators → reusable hook formulas

Method note (applies to all sections): verbatim lines below were pulled on 2026-10-29 by listing each channel's Shorts tab with yt-dlp (up to the 500 most recent Shorts), sorting by view count, and reading the first ~9 s of YouTube's auto-captions. Where a channel had no captions (Zack D. Films, Jenny Hoyos, some Kurzgesagt/Veritasium/Ryan Trahan/Dr Julie), the first 12 s of audio were transcribed locally with faster-whisper (small.en). Auto-captions have no punctuation and occasional mis-hearings ("Zach D films", "growler bear"); I kept them as heard and lightly punctuated. View counts are YouTube's rounded figures on that date. "Most-viewed" = most-viewed among the last ≤500 Shorts on the channel's Shorts tab, not necessarily all-time.

## 1. Verbatim opening lines per creator and the pattern they follow

### Takeaway
Across ~70 top Shorts from 20 channels, the first sentence almost always does one of six jobs: (a) points at an on-screen object ("This is…"), (b) asks the exact question the viewer would ask ("What would happen if…"), (c) states a concrete premise/challenge in first person ("I tested / I'm going to…"), (d) drops the viewer into the middle of a physical action (Zack D.), (e) gives an imperative warning ("Never do this…", "Don't make this mistake"), or (f) states a counter-intuitive fact as a flat declarative. Greeting/intro lines never appear. Hooks are short: a 1,178-Short study found a median of 11 words.

### Inferences
- "This is…" / "These…" deictic openers are disproportionately common in Shorts (12.6% of million-view Shorts hooks vs 4.3% of long-form — [Overseeros](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/youtube-shorts-hooks-study)); the creators above (Rober, Cleo, MinuteEarth, AiP, Ryan, Phil) confirm it: the sentence is a caption for the first frame.
- Two-beat structure is near-universal: beat 1 = the hook (≤ ~11 words), beat 2 = an escalation or contrast inside the first 3–5 s ("…but this is 30,000 pounds", "Wait, what?", "Probably not. But I'm going to show you how"). That matches Jenny Hoyos's hook + foreshadow rule (Section 3).
- The very top numbers (Rober 600M+, Vsauce 325M, Ryan 200M) come from openers where the visual alone is the hook and the spoken line just names it.

### Gaps
- Wendover Productions and Pursuit of Wonder: their Shorts tabs returned no items via yt-dlp (they post few or no Shorts), so no verbatim lines.
- Dr Karan Raj: his YouTube Shorts channel ([Dr Karan](https://youtube.com/channel/UCc-Yj3lMj3s_n1tqcGIzQ6g)) was found but the auto-captions cut off after a few words; his TikTok (@dr.karanr) originals were not accessible. Partial lines are in Section 2.
- Zack D. Films' first frame may carry on-screen text not captured by audio transcription.

## 2. Formulas specific to body / medical curiosity Shorts

### Takeaway
Medical/body Shorts use five recurring openers: (1) the FAQ framing "One of the common questions we get…" (IHA), (2) "let's pretend you…" second-person scenario on a real specimen (IHA), (3) "Some of you might have [weird body variant]" identity callout (IHA), (4) a danger-sign imperative "If your [everyday object] looks like this, get out" / "Never do what X is doing" (Dr Karan, Doctor Mike), and (5) "This person is about to reveal the shocking truth…" stitch/reaction setup (Dr Karan). The "Here's what's actually happening when…" phrasing suggested in the brief did NOT appear in the top-5 IHA Shorts I sampled.

### Inferences
- Body Shorts lean on (a) a real specimen/visual that is itself shocking (cadaver, blood-spotted toilet roll, chest cleft), so the spoken line can be calm and descriptive; and (b) the second person ("you got bit", "some of you might have", "if your toilet paper") which turns anatomy into "this is about your body".
- Danger imperatives ("never", "get out", "do not eat") are the doctor-creator signature; they borrow the authority of the white coat. Without that authority (a faceless channel), the same line reads as fearmongering — see failure conditions in Section 6.

## 3. Jenny Hoyos's hook rules (precise, with sources)

### Takeaway
Jenny's documented rule set: hook in ~1 s (aim within the first 3 s), hook + foreshadow = 2 lines ≤ 3 s, the hook must be understood visually with the sound off, story driven by "but/therefore" changes, end right after the promised payoff, low reading level. "Start at the climax" and "'but' at the end of the hook" are NOT phrased that way in the primary sources I found — the "but" rule is about the body of the story.

### Cited Findings
- "I really do think you have one second to hook someone, especially on Shorts." — [YouTube Official Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/)
- Formula named on the YouTube blog: shock, intrigue, satisfy — [YouTube Official Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/)
- "I do a hook, and then I foreshadow, which is 2 lines, and that is usually 3 seconds or less." — [Creator Science podcast (Jay Clouse)](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- "The hook needs to be very visual. You need to understand it without even listening to it." / "The first thing is the visual. Easily… and then I figure out how I'm gonna accompany that visual." — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- "But therefore, but therefore storytelling, simply put, is just lots of change in the story." — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- Example: hook "McDonald's banned this item" → foreshadow "I'm gonna make it at home then convince them to put it back on the menu." — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- Example: "My mom's never had a Mother's Day gift, so I'm gonna change that." — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- Puts scripts in a readability checker to keep them concise — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- Thumbnails "not important because 99.9% of the views are coming from the Shorts feed" — [YouTube Official Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/)
- Secondary summaries (not verbatim-verified): "'But' in your story stops someone from scrolling"; "No more than three objects in frame", centered; "watching on mute you understand it"; aim for ≤ 5th-grade reading level; end immediately after the payoff ("Whatever you say you're going to do, end it right after"); ~34 s for her top videos — [FinalLayer](https://finallayer.com/blog/jenny-hoyos-the-18-year-old-phenomenon-who-achieved-viral-success). Her structure as hook → foreshadow (≤3 s) → but/therefore narrative → twist ending — [Medium summary](https://medium.com/@ammarsheikhs/the-secrets-of-youtube-short-sensation-jenny-hoyos-e6026a855e9b)

### Inferences
- In her own top Shorts the spoken hook is a challenge/deal ("I'll give you a hundred dollars if…") or is absent entirely (Dumpling Challenge), confirming visual-first.
- For a Korean explainer writer the operational version is: line 1 = what we see/claim (≤ ~1 s of speech), line 2 = the promise of where this goes, both done by 3 s; then alternate "그런데 / 그래서".

### Gaps
- "Start the story at the climax" and "put 'but' at the end of the hook" could not be traced to a primary Jenny quote; I did not find her Colin & Samir episode transcript. Treat them as community paraphrase.

## 4. Second 0–1: first frame + on-screen text + voice pairing

### Takeaway
The dominant pairing is "voice names what the first frame shows": the first frame is a single clear object or event, the voice line is a caption for it ("This is a slinky", "This milk is in a bag", "These two chain ladders were dropped…"), and the title/on-screen text restates the question. Galloway's data frames second 0–1 as a thumbnail: viewed-vs-swiped-away (VVSA) under 60% rarely does well.

### Cited Findings
- 3.3B views / 5,400 Shorts / 33 channels (Apr 2023): Shorts with < 60% VVSA "rarely performed well"; best typically 70–90%; treat the intro like a thumbnail and "make your first second really punchy." Longer Shorts did better on average; likes/comments/shares showed "no strong relationships". — [Paddy Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
- Jenny: hook must be understood "without even listening to it"; visual first, then words to accompany it — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/); keep frame simple (≤3 objects, centered — secondary) — [FinalLayer](https://finallayer.com/blog/jenny-hoyos-the-18-year-old-phenomenon-who-achieved-viral-success)
- Concrete pairings observed (from transcripts + titles): Rober — object on screen + "This is a [object]" + title "This Slinky Can Walk Forever!" ([YouTube](https://youtube.com/shorts/KIlYZivq_H4)); Veritasium — rig on screen + "What will happen to this weight if you cut the green rope?" + same title ([YouTube](https://youtube.com/shorts/itSkBESLZeY)); Dr Karan — blood-spotted roll on screen + "If your toilet paper looks like this…" ([YouTube](https://youtube.com/shorts/KoEw2iQ0WFM)); Zack D. — 3D animation already mid-impact + narration of that impact ([YouTube](https://youtube.com/shorts/CRGWDiIgMZ4)).
- MrBeast's production guide: the opening exists to prove the title/thumbnail expectation will be met — [Simon Willison summary of the guide](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Sep/15/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)

### Inferences
- Voice and picture should be redundant at second 0 (muted viewers get it from the frame, listening viewers get it from the voice), then diverge after the foreshadow.

### Gaps
- I could not inspect the actual first frames/on-screen text of each Short (no frame capture done); pairings above are inferred from transcripts and titles. Claims like "incomplete visual hooks get 2.4× higher 3-s retention than text overlays (VidMob 2025)" appear only in vendor blogs without the primary report — [Virvid](https://virvid.ai/blog/first-3-seconds-hook-faceless-shorts-2026); not verified.

## 5. Published analyses ranking hook types

### Takeaway
No rigorous public ranking of Shorts hook types by retention exists. The best available: Galloway (VVSA threshold, not hook type), a 1,178-Short corpus study (descriptive: questions and "this/that" openers more common in Shorts, but mostly a creator-style effect), and a long-form BrightBean score ranking (Result-first > Contrarian > Story > Stat > Question). Everything else is vendor content marketing.

### Cited Findings
- Overseeros: 1,178 Shorts + 2,788 long-form hooks from 1M+ view videos, 1,032 channels. Shorts hooks median 11 words; questions 23.4% (vs 11.8% long-form); WH-question starts 19.5%; "this/these/that/those" starts 12.6% (vs 4.3%); second-person 36.8%; digits 9.8%. Within the same 74 channels making both formats, the question gap vanished (10.8% vs 10.6%) — winner-only, no causation. — [Overseeros](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/youtube-shorts-hooks-study)
- BrightBean: 10,000 long-form videos (Jan 2025–Jan 2026, 10K–1M-sub channels), proprietary 30-s retention score: Result First 78, Contrarian Claim 76, Story Setup 75, Shocking Stat 74, Question 73, Identity Callout 72, Authority Open 70, Social Proof 68; combined two-type hooks 79 vs 73 single. Proprietary score, long-form only. — [BrightBean](https://brightbean.xyz/blog/youtube-hook-types-analysis/)
- Galloway/Gileta: 3.3B views, VVSA 70–90% for best Shorts — [Thread Reader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
- "Immediate hook in the first 2 seconds retains 19% more viewers (Zebracat 2025)" — cited by vendor blogs only — [Virvid](https://virvid.ai/blog/first-3-seconds-hook-faceless-shorts-2026); unverified.

### Inferences
- Combining two types (e.g. deictic "This is X" + contrarian "but it's actually Y") is the most defensible recommendation: it matches the BrightBean combined-hook result and what Rober/MinuteEarth/Phil actually do.

### Gaps

## 6. Hook formula taxonomy (shape templates for Korean adaptation)

### Takeaway
Twelve reusable shapes cover essentially every top opener above. Each is given as an abstract template, mechanism, failure condition and a Korean shape example (words are illustrative, not translations).

### Cited Findings
(Each formula's evidence = the verbatim lines cited in Sections 1–2.)

| # | Formula | Template | Evidence | Mechanism | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Deictic + escalation | "This is [normal X]. But this is [extreme X]." | Rober slinky/Jello; MinuteEarth grolar/pizzly | Frame + caption = instant comprehension; contrast creates scale gap | The "extreme" isn't visible or isn't extreme enough |
| F2 | Viewer's own question | "What would happen if [absurd but imaginable act]?" + 2–3 rapid sub-guesses | Cleo hole 93M; Veritasium rope 52M | Forces a private prediction; sub-guesses make viewer pick one | Question is generic ("Ever wondered about sleep?") or answer is guessable |
| F3 | Conditional physical surprise | "If you [simple action], [impossible-seeming result]." | Vsauce thermite 325M; Action Lab laser | Cause→effect promise; viewer waits to see it | Result is described but not shown |
| F4 | In-medias-res action | "[Subject] [violent/vivid verb] [object]; now every time [consequence]…" | Zack D. shark/toucan/eagle 50–58M | Skips setup; brain needs to know how it got here and how it ends | Narration lags the visual or the subject isn't clear in 1 s |
| F5 | Hidden thing in the everyday | "Something in your [everyday place] [does dramatic X] [right now/every hour]." | Kurzgesagt antimatter; Dr Karan toilet paper | Proximity + threat/wonder; "your" makes it personal | The reveal is trivial or the claim is overstated (trust loss) |
| F6 | Myth reversal | "[Common belief] isn't [A]. It's actually [surprising B]." | MinuteEarth puppy; Hank microwave | Prediction error; viewer re-checks own belief | Belief isn't actually common in the audience |
| F7 | Absolute claim with one exception | "Every [X] has always [Y]… except for [tiny exception]." | Hank "30 minutes in 1971" 19M | Universal statement + anomaly = must know the exception | The exception is boring or unverifiable |
| F8 | First-person test/premise | "I [tested/bought/tried] [X] to see if [claim]. Starting with…" | Sambucha 20–22M; Veritasium shade balls | Clear contract + list engine; skeptic stance invites viewer to judge | No real test footage; result is predictable |
| F9 | Danger imperative | "Never/Don't [common act]. [Authority] [why]." or "If your [object] looks like this, [act now]." | Doctor Mike 66M; Dr Julie 33M; Dr Karan 12M | Loss aversion + authority | Speaker lacks credibility; platform health-claim risk; overused |
| F10 | Shared-experience callout | "Have you ever [specific mundane moment / seen these at the store]? Let's figure out [how/why]." | chemteacherphil 134M; Ryan 119M | Recognition ("that's me") then curiosity | The moment is not specific enough to recognize |
| F11 | Identity callout (body) | "Some of you have [rare body trait]" / "People with [trait] have…" | IHA "8 percent" 3.7M; Dr Karan grey eyes 13M | Self-check reflex; viewers test themselves | Trait is too rare (no self-check) or too common (no surprise) |
| F12 | Misdirect | "[Object] does something cool, and I'm not talking about [obvious cool thing]." / "I thought this was [A], but it turned out to be [B]." | chemteacherphil 80M & 45M | Closes the obvious explanation, opens a new gap | The real thing is less impressive than the decoy |

Supporting rules from creators/analysts:
- Hook + foreshadow by 3 s (Jenny) — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- First second treated like a thumbnail; VVSA ≥70% target — [Galloway](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
- Median Shorts hook ≈ 11 words — [Overseeros](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/youtube-shorts-hooks-study)

### Inferences
Korean shape examples (illustrative, for adapting the shape, not translations):
- F1: "이건 보통 [X]예요. 그런데 이건 [X의 1만 배]예요." / 평서형: "이건 평범한 [X]. 그런데 이건 [극단 X]."
- F2: "[터무니없는 행동]을 하면 어떻게 될까? [추측1]? [추측2]? 아니면 [추측3]?"
- F3: "[간단한 행동]만 하면 [믿기 힘든 결과]가 생긴다."
- F4: "[주어]가 [격한 동사]하는 순간, [결과]. 그 뒤로 [곤란한 상태]." (설명 없이 장면 한가운데서 시작)
- F5: "지금 당신 [부엌/몸/폰] 안에서 [극적인 일]이 일어나고 있다."
- F6: "[통념]은 [A]가 아니다. 사실 [의외의 B]에 가깝다."
- F7: "[모든 X]는 언제나 [Y]였다. 딱 [작은 예외]만 빼고."
- F8: "[주장]이 진짜인지 [X]를 직접 해 봤다. 먼저 [첫 번째]부터."
- F9: "[흔한 행동], 절대 하지 마세요." / "[물건]이 이렇게 생겼다면 바로 [행동]하세요."
- F10: "[아주 구체적인 일상 순간], 겪어 본 적 있죠? 왜 그런지 알아보자."
- F11: "몇몇 사람은 [희귀 신체 특징]이 있다. 지금 확인해 보세요."
- F12: "이 [물건]은 정말 신기한 걸 한다. [뻔한 신기함] 말고." / "[A]인 줄 알았는데 [B]였다."
- Body/medical channels without a doctor on camera should prefer F5/F6/F11 with a striking visual over F9 imperatives (authority gap).
- Universal failure modes: greetings or channel intros; a hook whose payoff isn't shown; spoken hook that contradicts or lags the first frame; questions with an obvious answer; overclaiming that the body can't deliver (trust drops, and for health topics also platform risk).

### Gaps
- No creator-level A/B data tying a specific formula to retention; the taxonomy is derived from top-performer lines (survivorship bias) plus the descriptive corpus study.
